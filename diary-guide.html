Creating Your Account

The Cull Diary requires a free account to sync your records securely across devices. Open the diary from the main First Light app by tapping Cull Diary in the header.

After creating your account, check your email for a confirmation link. Once confirmed, sign in with your email and password.

If you forget your password, tap Forgot password? on the sign-in screen. Enter the email for your account and use the link in the reset email to choose a new password (check spam if nothing arrives).

Adding a New Entry

Tap the + button or the Add Entry tab to open the full entry form. A Cull · Blank day · Sighting selector at the top chooses what you are logging (a helper line explains each), with Cull selected by default. A cull entry has an optional Photo then eight numbered sections — 1 Species, 2 Sex, 3 Date & Time, 4 Location, 5 Shot Details, 6 Carcass, 7 Larder inspection, 8 Notes. Blank day and Sighting show fewer, tailored fields (see 03b and 03d).

Undo a save

Right after you save a new entry, the success toast carries a short-lived Undo — a 6-second window to reverse a mistaken save (wrong species, an accidental double-tap) with a single tap. It appears on new culls and blank days saved online, from both the full form and Quick entry.

Red Deer saved Undo

Tap Undo and the entry is deleted again — photo and all — with a “Entry removed” confirmation. Editing an existing entry shows a plain “Entry updated” instead (there is nothing to undo), and entries queued offline keep their own sync toast. If the window lapses or the undo can’t reach the server, you’ll see “Could not undo — the entry is saved” — open the entry and delete it from there instead.

Blank day (outing, no shot)

When you have been out but did not take a shot — and still want a dated record on the right ground — use Blank day instead of a full cull. Open Add entry, then use the in-form control to switch to the blank-day flow (you will not pick species, sex, or carcass details).

Log date & time, location and ground as usual, and add notes if you like. Photos are not part of a blank day in the same way as a cull entry — the list uses a no shot / landscape motif so you can spot it quickly.

In the diary list, the card title is Blank day with the ground and your note preview. Species filter must be on All — a blank day is not a deer species, so a species chip hides it. Search picks up blank, blank day, outing, and no shot in addition to your ground and notes.

Stats: Cull/carcass and species KPIs on Stats focus on actual culls. Blank days are listed separately (outings without a shot) so your season numbers stay meaningful for the larder and returns.

Pest Control species

The species picker keeps its six deer buttons; below them sits a collapsed Pest Control ▸ button. Tap it to reveal Fox, Rabbit, Grey Squirrel, Pigeon, Corvid and Wild Boar. If you never shoot pests, you never see them — the deer workflow is unchanged.

Picking a pest species slims the form down automatically:

• Fox — sex becomes ♂ Dog / ♀ Vixen, and the age classes include Cub. No carcass weight, no tag number, no gralloch checklist.
• Rabbit, grey squirrel, pigeon, corvid — count-only: no sex, no age, no weight, no tag, no gralloch. Date, location, shot details and notes still apply — a pest entry can be saved in seconds.

For those four, a How many stepper appears (section 2). Type the number shot in one outing — tap the figure to key it straight in (e.g. 200 pigeons) rather than logging them one by one; it defaults to 1. That count flows into your diary Total, Stats, CSV and PDFs, and into syndicate tallies. Fox and wild boar are sexed, so they are logged one entry each.

Pest entries appear in the diary list and cull map with their own colours, count in your Stats, and can be attributed to a syndicate like any cull. They are excluded from larder and game-dealer documents (deer-only paperwork — section 10a) and have no cull targets — they are counts, not plan species.

Legal: pigeons and corvids are shot under general licences that change annually and differ by UK country (England, Wales, Scotland, NI). Read the current licence for your country before you shoot — this app quotes no licence numbers, as they go stale.

Sightings — live deer seen

A sighting records deer you saw but did not shoot — it builds a picture of what is using your ground. In Add entry, switch the top selector to Sighting. Sightings are deer only and kept in your own private log — never shared with a syndicate, never counted as culls, and never in targets or dealer paperwork.

A sighting captures: Species, a How many seen composition (bucks / does / young / unknown steppers — at least one), optional Behaviour chips, Location (pin / GPS / ground), date & time, an optional photo, and notes. No sex, carcass, calibre or larder fields.

Open your log from the Sightings strip in the diary header (it also opens after you save one). The Sightings view lists them with species chips and a newest / oldest sort, plus a List / Trends toggle — Trends shows totals, a bucks-to-does ratio, animals by month, and a by-species breakdown. The hotspot map in List view can be narrowed by time of day (dawn / day / dusk / night — judged against sunrise and sunset at the pin) and by month. CSV and PDF buttons export a sightings report. Tap a card to open it, then Edit or Delete from the detail view; a small thumbnail shows when you attached a photo.

On the Stats cull map, a Culls / Sightings / Both toggle overlays sightings as density circles (green→red, sized by animals seen) so you can see which areas are getting the most deer activity. On Stands, each stand shows a Seen here card, and the Season Plan shows seen vs culled per species this season.

Log one from a trail camera. The form’s At a stand? picker lists your high seats and — under Cameras & feed sites — the trail cams, feeders and licks you have mapped on your grounds. Pick the camera and the sighting takes its pin, ground and name in one tap, so working through an SD card becomes select, count, save. The sightings CSV carries a Seen from column to match.

Slice the log by source. Once any sighting is linked, a Seen from dropdown appears beside the season filter in the Sightings view — pick one camera or one seat and read just its story. The same story is one tap away on the map: see Camera intel on the map.

Sightings vs blank days: a blank day records an outing where you took no shot (for hours-stalked returns); a sighting records deer you watched. They are separate logs — use whichever fits.

Editing & Deleting

From the detail view, tap Edit to reopen the full form with all fields pre-filled. Make your changes and save. The entry updates in place — no duplicate created.

Tap Delete to permanently remove an entry. You'll be asked to confirm. Deleted entries cannot be recovered.

Warning: Deletion is permanent. If you need a record for compliance, export the entry as PDF before deleting.
7

Season & Ground Management

The diary organises entries by stalking season — by default August–July, or twelve months from your own chosen start month (see Season start month below). Use the season dropdown in the diary header to switch between seasons.

Grounds (permissions / estates) let you tag entries by where you stalk. To add or review saved grounds:

1. Open Stats → on the Season Plan card tap Set targets or Edit (wording depends on whether you already have targets).
2. Open the By ground tab and use Add ground to name a permission.
3. Saved grounds then appear as tappable options in the entry form’s Permission / Ground field.

Tip: Grounds persist across seasons, and you can remove one from the targets sheet when you no longer need it. Beyond a name, a ground can also be drawn on a map — boundary, no-shoot zones, tracks and markers — see Mapping your grounds below.

Season start month

By default the diary year runs August–July, so “2025–2026 Season” means Aug 2025 – Jul 2026. If your ground runs a different year — many Deer Management Groups plan April–March — set your own start month via the gear icon in the header → Profile → Season starts → Edit. Your entries re-sort into the new seasons instantly and losslessly, and you can change the setting back at any time — nothing about the entries themselves changes, only how they are grouped into seasons.

Targets caveat: cull targets keep their season names (e.g. “2025-26”) but count against the new dates after a change — a target you set for Aug–Jul becomes a target for Apr–Mar, so progress against it can shift. The app shows the old and new dates in a warning before anything is applied.

Syndicates are separate: each syndicate has its own season start month, which its managers set from the syndicate Manage sheet. Your personal setting drives your diary list, stats, targets and personal exports; syndicate cards, team targets, allocations and team exports follow that syndicate’s setting. The same cull can therefore legitimately sit in a differently-dated “2025-26” in the two places — that is correct, not a bug.

With a January start, seasons are single calendar years and are labelled that way (“2026 Season”).

Mapping your grounds

A ground can be more than a name in a list — you can draw it on a map: its boundary and acreage, no-shoot zones, tracks and rides, and point markers for the things that matter (trail cams, gates, the larder, a wallow). Mapped grounds power the acreage figures, the cull-density-per-100 ha note on Stats, and auto-suggest the right ground when you drop a pin.

Two ways in. Either open Account & settings (the gear) → the Grounds card → Manage (its line reads e.g. “3 grounds · 2 mapped”), which opens the My grounds sheet; or, on the Stands map, use the floating bar’s Grounds button. Before you have drawn anything, the map shows a Draw your ground boundary → invite.

Locate — an honest blue dot. Locate paints a you-are-here beacon with a ring showing the fix’s real accuracy (“±8 m”). It waits for a genuine satellite fix rather than grabbing the phone’s first answer, and if all it can get is mast-based positioning — which can sit a kilometre out — it says so and draws nothing, because a confidently wrong dot is worse than none. Record pins are gated the same way: GPS fills a cull or sighting location only once the fix is inside 150 m.

Drawing a boundary. Choose Draw boundary, line the crosshair up on each corner of the ground in turn and tap ✚ Add point; Close ring joins the last corner to the first and shows the enclosed area. Drag a corner to nudge it, or tap it twice to remove it. Prefer to walk it? The boots button drops a corner roughly every 15 m as you walk the line — keep the screen awake. A boundary can hold up to 500 points; if it crosses itself the app still saves but warns you to check the corners. Across all grounds, an account holds up to 80 drawn features — boundaries, zones, lines and markers together.

The other shapes. The same editor also draws:

• No-shoot zones — ground you never take a shot toward (a road, houses, a footpath), drawn as its own outline.
• Lines — routes typed as Ride, Track, Footpath or Compartment (or a plain line); each shows its length.
• Markers — a single point typed as Trail cam, Parking, Structure, Gate, Larder or Wallow (or a plain marker). Pan the map, tap Place marker, then drag the dot to fine-tune.

Manage grounds

Switching & managing. The Grounds button lists each ground with a colour dot and its acreage (or “not mapped yet — tap to draw”); tap one to fly the map to it. Manage grounds (or the Edit link) turns on a rename and a delete icon per ground — deleting asks first (Cancel / Delete anyway) and, importantly, keeps your high seats and diary entries; they simply lose the ground tag. Individual parcels, zones, lines and markers each have Edit and a ✕ that needs a second Sure? tap.

Measure without saving. The Measure tool (the ＋ menu, or Measure distance & area in the sheet) is a scratch pad — tap corners (or walk them with the boots button) for a live distance along the path, then Close for area to flip the readout to acreage and perimeter. Nothing is saved, and it works fully offline.

Import & export. In the My grounds sheet, Import KML/GPX brings a boundary in from another mapping tool (it arrives as its own new ground, named from the file); Export GeoJSON and Export GPX download all your shapes — with your high seats riding along as points/waypoints — as first-light-grounds.geojson / .gpx.

Online for edits: drawing, importing and renaming grounds need a live connection (“Still offline — … needs signal”). Once synced, your boundaries stay viewable offline on every map in the app.

The cull map

Every entry with a pin appears on the interactive map. Three control groups sit with it:

• Map / Satellite — the base layer (your choice is remembered).
• Culls / Sightings / Both — which layer you are looking at.
• Pins / Heat — show culls as individual pins, or as a heat map of density (this toggle steps aside when you are viewing Sightings only).

Pins are branded. Each cull is a teardrop in its species colour with a ♂ / ♀ glyph, so colour and shape carry the meaning. Zoom out and nearby pins cluster into a count tinted by the dominant species; when you have culls on more than one date, earlier ones fade so the freshest read brightest (the caption notes “fainter = earlier”). Tap a pin for a little popup — species, sex, date, weight, tag, placement, location — with an Open entry › link straight into that record.

Reading the heat map. In Heat mode the caption tells you what warmth means: “Denser = where you cull most” for culls, “Warmer & larger = more deer seen” for sightings, and “Warmer & denser = more activity here” when both layers are on together.

Species chips under the map do double duty: they choose which pins show and they rebuild all the stats and charts below for that species (or All). They are separate from the Diary tab’s species filter (which only narrows the list) — set the same species here if you want the numbers to match what you were reading in the list.

Missing a pin? If some entries were saved without GPS, the map’s No GPS figure becomes tappable and opens a recovery sheet listing them. Tap Place on a row, drop the pin where it belongs, and it saves back to the entry (“Location saved”) — the same flow works for sightings, and it is the quickest way to backfill locations onto older records.

Fullscreen. The expand button opens the map full-screen and slides the layer toggles and species chips into an on-map tray so you keep every control; tap it again to drop back to the card. When nothing is pinned yet the map shows “No mapped locations yet” with a nudge to use Pin or GPS as you log.

Charts & breakdowns

Below the plan / exports area, open Charts & breakdowns to reveal sections such as:

• Monthly cull — activity through the season; the twelve columns run in your season order, starting at your season start month (Aug → Jul by default)
• Cull by ground — when you use grounds
• Species and sex splits
• Age class, shooter, carcass destination
• Calibre and shot distance (when those fields are filled on entries)

Each block respects your current season on Stats and the map species chip (see above). Empty sections show a short “no data” message.

Always visible on Stats

• Season progress hero — the adaptive headline and quiet strip described above; its figures follow the map species chip once you change it from All
• Season Plan — targets vs actuals; the Overview / ground chips above the plan choose which permission’s entries count as “actual” (separate from the map species filter)
• Syndicates card — shared team targets when you belong to a syndicate (see step 13)
• Exports — personal CSV / PDF / Summary / Larder; dealer declarations (step 10a)

How each export works

CSV, PDF, and Larder Book open the same bottom sheet: Season, Permission / Ground, then Entries matching selection (live count) before you generate. All Seasons appears when your history is loaded. UTF-8 CSV opens cleanly in Excel; PDF is a simple formatted list; Larder Book is a dedicated PDF for larder / game-handling style records (entries marked Left on hill are not included, and Pest Control entries never are — the Larder Book covers deer only).

The CSV includes Latitude / Longitude columns (populated when you saved a GPS pin or used the GPS button on Location) and Outing start / Outing finish times — useful for DMG and government funding reports that need GPS evidence or hours-stalked figures. The single-entry PDF and the diary PDF list the outing window in each entry's meta line; other PDFs (Larder, Game Dealer, Consignment, Season Summary) are deliberately unchanged.

Summary opens a different sheet (same season + ground idea) to build the Season Summary PDF — header stats, species breakdown, plan progress, and a full entry table — good for estates or DMGs.

Elsewhere in the app

• Entry detail — PDF vs Game dealer PDF (see declarations & consignments below).
• Diary list → Select — multi-select entries for CSV, Dealer PDF (consignment declaration for several carcasses at once), or Delete.
• Syndicate exports (managers only) — team-wide CSV / PDF / Summary / Larder under Stats; see step 13.

Trained hunter declarations & consignments

These are not the same as the big Stats exports above — they are legal-style PDFs built from your entries for game dealers and food-safety traceability.

One carcass — trained hunter declaration. Open any entry → Game dealer PDF. That downloads a trained hunter declaration for that single cull, using the details you saved (species, shot, destination, tag, abnormalities, etc.). The plain PDF button next to it is only a simple diary record of the same entry — useful for your files, but different in purpose.

Many carcasses, one delivery — consignment declaration. On the diary list, tap Select, tick every entry that is leaving in the same consignment to the game dealer, then Dealer PDF. The app produces one PDF covering the whole batch (one declaration block and signature line for the delivery). This matches how assimilated Reg. (EC) No 853/2004 paperwork often expects one declaration per consignment rather than one PDF per carcass. Entries marked Left on hill are excluded; if none of your selection qualify, the app will tell you.

Pest entries are excluded. Declarations and consignments are wild-game (deer) documents: pest entries show no Game dealer PDF button, and any pests ticked in a selection are left out of the consignment — if nothing else qualifies, the app tells you.

These PDFs support your records workflow — they do not replace professional legal or food-business advice.

Tip: If you are offline, exports that need a full history may fall back to the season already loaded on the device — the app will say so in a toast.

Offline Mode

The diary works without signal. When you're offline:

• New entries are saved to a local queue on your device
• A banner shows how many entries are queued and storage used
• When signal returns, entries sync automatically
• If storage runs low, photos are stripped from queued entries first to save space (you're told at the time)

Note: Photos are large. If you're offline for a long stretch and browser storage runs tight, the app strips photos from queued entries first; if that still isn't enough it will drop the oldest queued entries (telling you how many), and in the worst case clear the queue — so sync back up when you can rather than banking a big backlog offline.

Account & Privacy

Your diary data is stored securely in Supabase with row-level security, so no other user or syndicate member can see your entries. The site operator can access data only where necessary to run the service, fix a problem, or meet a legal obligation.

Tap the gear icon in the header of the Diary or Stats tab (or the Account & settings button at the bottom of Stats) to open the settings sheet:

• Profile — edit your display name (used on your account card, PDF signatures, and in syndicates), change your password, set your season start month (see section 07a), and choose the deer on your ground (which species to surface)
• Units — switch distances between metres and yards and larder weights between kilograms and pounds; your records stay metric underneath, so everyone’s figures still compare
• Grounds — Manage your permissions and their boundaries (see Mapping your grounds)
• App — the version you are running with a Check button for updates, and your offline sync status (entries saved without signal) with a manual Sync now
• Sign out — signs you out on this device
• Delete account — permanently removes your account and all diary data

For full details, read the Privacy Policy.

Syndicates — full walkthrough

Syndicates let a small team share a ground and run it together: the shared ground on a live map, outing bookings with a scored week planner, a team thread and field book, group cull targets, and optional push notifications. Access is invite-only (link or short code). Your own diary entries stay private — a syndicate only ever sees tallies you attribute to it. The layouts below are HTML mocks matched to the real Cull Diary — not PNG screenshots, but the same labels and flow.

Online — creating, joining, and syncing syndicates needs a live session. Sign in first.

The Syndicate tab

Syndicate has its own tab in the bottom bar, between Stands and Stats. A gold dot on the tab — visible from any screen — means something is waiting: new messages, a booking to approve, or a join request. The page reads top to bottom like a visit:

• Masthead — the syndicate’s name, the member strip (faces, join requests, and People › for managers), a scale line (grounds · acres · seats · when the outlook was last scored, with ↻ to refresh), and Manage ›.
• Best window this week — the week’s best free chance across every shared ground: score, seat, day, window, from-time, wind and moon, with Book ›. Once you hold a booking, a You’re out line sits underneath with a calendar button.
• The ground — the shared map (satellite or OS), ground chips to dive into one ground when the syndicate spans several, and seat pins that score right now — tap a pin for its seven-day ribbon.
• Bookings — section 13h.
• Messages and the field book — section 13g.
• Season targets and, for managers, Syndicate settings — section 13i.

The Syndicates card on Stats stays as the numbers view — team targets and progress. Its Open button jumps to the Syndicate tab; managers also get Manage for the sheet.

Messages & the field book

Each syndicate has a single rolling thread for team chat — manager announcements and member discussion mixed together, pinned announcements staying on top. Useful for "I'm out at North Wood today", "saw a roe doe by the larch", weekend plans, and so on. The thread is polled, not real-time — it refreshes when you open the Syndicate tab.

Where to find it — the Messages card on the Syndicate page, below the bookings. Unread messages light the gold dot on the Syndicate tab from any screen, and the Stats card shows an unread chip that jumps you straight to the page.

Posting — type into the box and tap Post. Messages are 500 characters max, cannot be edited after posting (write twice, post once), and are stamped with your member name and role at the time of posting (so a future role change does not rewrite history).

Pinned messages — managers can pin up to 3 messages at once (a indicator marks them). Useful for stuck-at-the-top notices like "weekend cull plan" or "ground rules". Soft-deleting a pinned message auto-unpins it. Members cannot pin or unpin.

Soft delete — your own messages can be removed; managers can remove anyone's. Removed messages no longer render but stay in the database with a deletion stamp, so the audit trail is preserved.

What you see — the thread shows the most recent 30 messages, with Show older to page further back. Everything stays in the database for audit.

The field book (ground notes) — separate from chat: lasting observations per ground, not conversation. “Fresh slots on the muddy ride”, “keep off the barley strip until harvest”. Notes start private to you (grey rule); Share turns the rule gold and every member sees it under that ground. Grounds with written notes sort first; who may add notes is a manager setting (13i).

Tip: The tab’s gold dot clears once you've read the thread, and reappears when a new message lands.

Bookings & Plan the week

Once a ground is shared, members book outings on it from the Syndicate page — dawn, dusk or all day. Depending on the share’s mode you book a seat or the whole ground (an “on foot” booking takes the whole ground either way). Double-booking is impossible — the app checks conflicts before the database does.

• Plan the week — the heart of the form: every seat scored across the next seven days, each cell the day’s best free window (score + dawn/dusk). Booked cells are struck out (a gold dot marks your own), the day’s best free sit wears a ring, closed days show ⊘. Tap a cell and the form snaps to exactly that seat, day and window.
• Booking further out — the day chips cover the scored week; Pick a date… books up to the syndicate’s horizon (a year by default). Beyond the week the booking is locked in now and the forecast fills in later.
• Seat pins on the map — tap one for its seven-day ribbon (same chips as a stand’s detail) and a Book › button; your own seats also get Stands › straight to their full detail.
• Approval or instant — the manager chooses (13i). Under approval your booking shows awaiting approval until a manager confirms; managers see the queue at the top of the bookings card, and can book through closed days.
• Who’s out — the next seven days of team outings, so nobody stalks into an occupied wood. Names can be made anonymous for members (13i).
• Yours — cancel your own upcoming outings, bring a guest where allowed, and export everything you’ve booked as a calendar file. Your next booking also headlines Your Next Sit on the Stands tab.

Tip: The Best window this week hero above the map and the ring in Plan the week agree — they are the same calculation: the highest-scoring window the whole team may actually still book.

Syndicate settings (manager)

The Syndicate settings card sits at the bottom of the page (managers only), collapsed to one line that lists every non-default choice. Open it for four groups of switches — each takes effect for the whole team immediately:

• Bookings — Confirmation (Instant / Approval), Fair-use cap (upcoming bookings each member may hold), Guests, Horizon (1 month / 3 months / season), Privacy (Names / Anonymous on others’ bookings).
• Community — who may post Messages, who may add Ground notes, Joining (You approve / Instant), Cull shouts on/off — the optional “Tell the syndicate” tick on a member’s entry form, which posts a one-line “Cull logged: Roe Deer on Home Wood” to Messages (species, sex and ground only — never the spot, the weight or the notes). Switching it off hides the tick for everyone.
• Invites — New invite — code + link mints both at once; the active list shows each code with uses left and expiry, plus Copy code, Copy link and Revoke. Revoking never affects people who already joined.
• Closed dates — shoot days, estate work: close a date or a whole run with an optional reason. Members can’t book them (they show ⊘ everywhere); you can. Reopen lifts them.

Push notifications

The bell chip on the masthead (Alerts) switches push notifications for this device — outline means tap to enable, gold On means tap to turn off. Four things buzz:

• a join request → the managers,
• a booking awaiting approval → the managers,
• a pinned announcement → every member,
• your approval → you, the new member.

Each device opts in separately (phone and desktop each have their own bell). On iPhone, notifications need the app installed to the home screen (iOS 16.4+) — in a plain Safari tab the bell simply doesn’t appear; that is iOS, not a fault. Delivery is fire-and-forget: if a push ever misfires, the booking or message it announced is entirely unaffected.

Stands & Deer Forecast

The Stands tab (third button on the bottom bar) stores your high seats and stalking spots — on one ground or on grounds counties apart — and scores each one's dawn and dusk windows for the next 7 days using weather, moon, rut and season, fetched fresh for that stand's exact location.

Adding a stand

Tap + Add stand. Give it a name, optionally link it to a saved ground, and set its location with the pin map or GPS — the same pin flow as a cull entry. Up to 150 stands; adding and editing need a connection (scores are cached for offline viewing). As you set the location, a small map preview shows the seat (Tap to adjust). You can also record the way it faces and add photos — see Facing, photos & where it sits below.

Bad winds is the important part: tap the compass directions that blow your scent from the stand toward where the deer come from. When the forecast wind blows from one of those directions during a window, that window's score is cut. Two seats 400 m apart share the same weather — bad winds are what make them score differently.

Reading the list

Stands group by ground. Each card shows a wind rose (red arc = your bad winds; gold arrow = today's forecast wind — arrow in the red arc means scent trouble), today's best window, and your cull count there. Above the list, a “Your next sit” hero calls out the single best upcoming window across all your stands — e.g. “Your next sit · tonight” with the seat, the time it opens, an activity read (High / Moderate / Low) and a wind / moon pill — the "which seat, tonight?" answer at a glance. For the whole week, switch to Plan week.

Sorting & filtering. Sort by Ground (grouped) or Best (today’s top score first), switch between Cards and a tighter Compact density, and — once you have a few seats — open Filter seats to show only a chosen ground or marker type, or to hide seat / marker names on the map.

Wind evidence. Once a stand has enough history, its card can back the theory with fact — e.g. “23 of 31 culls here came on a SW wind · today: SW ✓” — drawn from your own culls (or sightings) at that seat. It only appears when the record is solid enough to mean something.

Best seat this week

With two or more stands and a live forecast, a List / Plan week toggle appears. Plan week lays your seats against the next 7 days in a grid and rings the best seat for each day in gold — the fastest way to answer “which seat, which evening?”. A Best / Dawn / Dusk lens narrows it to the window you care about; each cell is a deer-activity score with its dawn/dusk tag, and tapping a day opens it.

Scent cones on the map

Switch the Stands view to the map and turn on Scent cones to draw a downwind wedge from each stand — the rough patch of ground where a deer could catch your scent on that day's forecast wind. Each cone is coloured by whether the wind betrays you there: green (clear — the wind is in your favour), amber (marginal), red (busted — your scent blows toward where the deer come from) or grey (too calm to call). A sit stepper walks the cones through the week’s actual windows — Now, Tonight · dusk, then each day’s dawn and dusk with the ‹ › arrows (tap the label to jump straight back to Now) — so you pick the sit where the wind sits right, and a small legend under the map names the colours. The cones read from the same forecast wind and bad-wind settings as the window scores, so they broadly track each stand's dawn/dusk ratings (they reflect one representative hour, so at the margins a cone and a score can differ).

Routes — which way to walk in

Scent decides more stalks on the walk in than at the seat. Draw your rides, tracks and footpaths on the ground map (Mapping your grounds → Draw line) and, with the scent lens on, every route you own tints by how the selected sit’s wind treats walking it: green — a clean way in exists; amber — workable, some legs run your scent ahead; red — the deer would wind you whichever end you start. Step the sit and the routes re-answer for that wind.

Tap a route for the split: the wind used (and which seat it was read at), both walking directions scored by how much of the walk runs your scent ahead of you, the one stretch to mind, and a plain verdict naming which end to start from.
